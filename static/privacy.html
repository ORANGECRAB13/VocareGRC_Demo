Vocare Translate

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 September 2026

The short version. Vocare Translate turns speech into translated speech. To do that, the audio you record while holding the speak button is sent to our server and to our speech and translation providers, converted to text, translated, and spoken back. There are no accounts, no sign-in, and no analytics or tracking SDKs. The Android app is free and shows a short advertisement from Google AdMob when you open it; section 2 explains exactly what that involves and how to limit it. Conversation transcripts are kept on your own device. A copy of the transcript text also exists on the server for the life of the running session process. Read on for exactly what that means.

2. What we collect and why

Microphone audio

When you hold the speak button, the app captures audio from your device microphone and streams it to our server over an encrypted WebRTC connection. This is the core function of the app: we cannot translate speech we cannot hear. The microphone is not opened when you are not in a translation session, and the app does not record in the background. On the Apple Watch companion, audio is recorded only for the duration of a single press and is uploaded as one complete recording when you release.

Transcripts of what is said

Your speech is converted to text (the "original") and that text is translated into the other language (the "translation"). Both texts are shown on screen, spoken aloud, and stored as the session transcript.

Language and session settings

The two languages you have selected, an optional display name for each speaker, an optional conversation topic, a randomly generated session code, and the session start time and duration. The display name and topic are free-text fields you fill in yourself — please do not enter information you would not want in a transcript.

Technical connection data

Establishing a real-time audio connection requires your device's network (IP) addresses to be exchanged with our server and with the relay service described in section 3. Our server writes operational logs — timestamps, session codes, connection events, recording sizes and audio levels, and error messages. These logs are for keeping the service working and are not used to profile you.

Advertising (Android app only)

The Android app is free and is supported by advertising. When you open the app, or bring it back to the foreground after a while, Google AdMob may show a full-screen advertisement that you can close using the control Google draws on it. We never show an advertisement while a translation session is in progress, and subscribers will not see advertisements once a paid tier exists. To serve and measure that advertisement, Google receives your device's advertising ID, your IP address (from which a coarse location is derived), basic device and app information, and whether you viewed or tapped the ad. Google never receives your microphone audio, your transcripts, or your translations. Where consent is required (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland) a consent form is shown before any ad is requested, and you can change your choice at any time under Settings → Privacy options; without consent, only non-personalised ads are requested. On any Android device you can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or turn off ad personalisation, under Settings → Google → Ads. Google's own handling of this data is described in its privacy policy at policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

What we deliberately do not collect

  • No account, email address, phone number, or password — the app has no sign-in at all.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting, attribution, or tracking SDKs are bundled in the app. The only third-party SDK is Google AdMob, in the Android app, for the advertisement described above; the iOS app currently contains no advertising.
  • No contacts, photos, calendar, location, or health data.
  • No payment information — the app does not take payments.

We do not sell or rent your data to anyone, and we do not share your audio, transcripts or translations with data brokers or advertisers, or use them for any purpose other than delivering the translation you asked for. The advertising SDK in the Android app never receives any of that; what it does receive is set out in section 2.

Please be aware: the app has no sign-in. A session's server-side copy is protected by its session code, which is long and randomly generated, and the app only ever lists sessions created on your own device. But the code is the only thing protecting that copy: anyone you share a session code with can view that session's transcript for as long as the server process is running. Share session codes only with people you intend to include in the conversation.

5. Your on-device history and how to clear it

Your conversation history lives in the app's own private storage on your device. It is not backed up to any account of ours, and we cannot read it.

  • iOS and watchOS: deleting the app removes its database and everything in it. This version of the app has no clear-history control of its own; deleting the app is the only way to remove it.
  • Android: Settings → Apps → Vocare Translate → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app.
  • If your device backs up app data (iCloud Backup, Google Backup), a copy of that history may be included in the backup, under Apple's or Google's own privacy terms.

To have the server-side copy of a session removed sooner than section 4 describes, contact us at the address in section 9 with the session code.

7. Legal basis for processing

Where the GDPR, UK GDPR, or a similar law applies:

  • Performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)) — processing your audio and transcripts is what delivers the translation service you have asked for.
  • Consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) — you grant microphone access explicitly through your operating system, and you may withdraw it at any time in your device settings, which stops all audio processing.
  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)) — keeping the service secure, available and free of abuse, using the operational logs described in section 2.

Spoken conversation can contain special-category information (Art. 9) if you choose to discuss, for example, health matters. We do not seek it out and we do not analyse transcripts for any purpose beyond producing the translation, but you should keep it in mind when deciding what to say.

Australian users: we handle personal information in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles.

For advertising in the Android app, non-personalised advertising is served on the basis of our legitimate interest in funding a free app, and personalised advertising only on the basis of your consent where the law requires it.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete, or export your personal information, to object to or restrict its processing, and to complain to your data protection authority. Because the app holds no account, we cannot look you up by name — a request about a specific session needs its session code. We do not sell personal information. In the Android app, the advertising SDK's use of your advertising ID for personalised advertising may amount to "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising as that term is used in California law; you can opt out by declining personalised ads under Settings → Privacy options, or by turning off ad personalisation in your device's Google settings.
